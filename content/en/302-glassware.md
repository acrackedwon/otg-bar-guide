## Without a stem

**Rocks / Old Fashioned**
Heavy base, wide mouth. For drinks served over a single large cube. Old Fashioned, Negroni.

**Highball**
Tall and straight. For long carbonated drinks. Gin & Tonic, highballs, Moscow Mule.

**Shot glass**
For spirits served straight.

## With a stem

**Coupe**
Wide, shallow bowl. Used for most drinks served without ice (straight up). Daiquiri, Sidecar.

**Nick & Nora**
Smaller than a coupe, tulip-shaped with an inward curve. It holds aroma and is hard to spill, so it is a favourite for Martini-style drinks.

**Martini / Cocktail glass**
The classic V shape. The wide mouth makes it easy to spill.

**Flute**
Tall and narrow. Sparkling wine and carbonated cocktails.

**Glencairn**
A small tulip-shaped glass **for tasting whisky**. The wide bowl opens the aroma up and the narrow rim gathers it.
Use it for single malts and for any whisky a guest is drinking neat to nose it.

**Snifter (brandy glass)**
Wide belly, narrow rim. Brandy, cognac, aged rum.
Traditionally cupped in the palm so body heat warms the spirit — **unlike every other glass, it is fine to hold this one by the bowl.** Pour little enough that it would not spill if the glass were laid on its side.

## Handling

- **Always hold the stem or the base.** Cupping the bowl leaves fingerprints and warms the drink.
- Never carry several glasses stacked in one hand. They chip when they knock together.
- When polishing a stemmed glass, **twisting the bowl against the stem will snap it**. A broken stem cuts badly.
