## Units

| Written | Means |
|---|---|
| A bare number (e.g. `45`) | Millilitres (ml) |
| `dash` | One shake of the bitters bottle |
| `bsp` | One full bar spoon |
| `drop` | A single drop from a dropper |
| `ea` | A count of items |

## Ice

| Written | Means |
|---|---|
| `5×5` | A 5×5cm cube. One per rocks glass |
| `4×4` | A 4×4cm cube |
| `3×3` | A 3×3cm cube |
| `machine ice` | The small cubes from the ice machine |

Bigger cubes melt slower. They go in stirred drinks and anything meant to be sipped.

## Syrups

| Name | Means |
|---|---|
| Simple syrup | Sugar and water, 1:1 |
| Rich syrup | Twice the sugar (2:1). Sweeter and thicker |
| Rich demerara | Rich syrup made with demerara sugar. Darker, with molasses character |
| Ginger syrup | Ginger syrup |
| Saline solution | Salt water. A few drops sharpen the line between sweet and sour |

## Techniques

**Throwing**
Pouring the liquid in a long stream between two tins to mix it. It aerates without the foam a shake creates.
Used where shaking would wreck the texture, as in a Bloody Mary.

**Floating**
Leaving the last ingredient **sitting on top** rather than mixed in — poured slowly over the back of a bar spoon.
The Laphroaig in a Penicillin is a float.

**Fill up**
Topping the rest of the glass with a carbonated mixer.

**Double strain**
Straining through a fine strainer as well as the Hawthorne.

## When a recipe has several versions

Old Fashioned, Negroni, Daiquiri and Sidecar each have more than one method.
**Ask if you are not sure which to use.** The choice depends on the guest, the stock and the glassware, so it is not one to make alone.
