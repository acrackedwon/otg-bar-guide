## Temperature

| Style | Temperature |
|---|---|
| Sparkling | 6–8°C |
| White | 8–12°C |
| Rosé | 8–12°C |
| Light red | 12–14°C |
| Full-bodied red | 15–18°C |

Room temperature is not the same as serving temperature. Our room is usually too warm even for red.

## Opening

1. Present the bottle with the label facing the guest before opening.
2. Cut the foil **below the lip of the neck**.
3. Drive the screw in straight, centred in the cork.
4. Use both steps of the lever and draw it out slowly. **It should not make a pop.**
5. Check the cork. A damp-cardboard or musty smell may mean the wine is corked — do not decide alone, tell someone.
6. Wipe the inside of the neck with linen.

## Pouring

1. Offer a taste to the guest who ordered.
2. Fill **only to the widest part of the bowl** — about a third for red, a little less for white. Never fill it up; the aroma needs the space.
3. **Twist the bottle slightly as you finish** so it does not drip.
4. Keep the label facing the guest.

## Handling the glass

- **Always hold the stem.** Holding the bowl warms the wine and leaves fingerprints.
- Hold the stem when setting it down in front of a guest too.
- Never carry several glasses stacked in one hand.
- Serve sparkling in a flute, tilting the glass as you pour so it does not foam over.
