## Shakers

**Boston shaker**
A large metal tin with a smaller tin (or pint glass). Fast and easy to clean, which is why most bars use it. It needs a separate strainer.

**Cobbler shaker**
Three pieces with a built-in strainer in the cap. No separate strainer needed, but it jams when it freezes up.

## Mixing glass

For stirred drinks. Thick glass with a wide mouth. Fill it with ice and stir with a bar spoon.

## Strainer

**Hawthorne**
The one with a spring. **This is the only strainer we use** — for the shaker and the mixing glass alike.

Sit it on the rim so the spring catches, hold the gate down with your index finger, and pour.
Pushing the gate forward or back with that finger changes the gap; close it up to hold back more ice.

## Fine strainer

A separate tool used **together with** the Hawthorne, not instead of it.

To **double strain**, hold the tin and Hawthorne in one hand and the fine strainer over the glass in the other, and pour through it. It catches ice shards and fragments of herb or fruit.

Use it for shaken drinks served without ice — Daiquiri, Gimlet, Coffee Martini. Ice shards left floating keep melting in the glass and water down the last mouthful.

## Bar spoon

A spoon with a long spiralled handle. Used for stirring, layering and small measures.

## Others

- **Muddler** — for pressing herbs and fruit
- **Peeler / channel knife** — for citrus peel
- **Ice pick** — for shaping ice
- **Squeezer** — for citrus
